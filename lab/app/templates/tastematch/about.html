1. The reviews

Taste Match follows {{ stats.data.channels if stats.data else "25" }} YouTube board game channels. Each review's audio is transcribed (Whisper, or YouTube's own captions), and the voices are told apart so a score goes to the host who said it.

A language model then reads each transcript and writes down every verdict: which game, which host, how much they liked it on a 1–5 scale, and the sentence that shows it. Every score links to its video and quote, so you can check it.

These scores are an automatic reading of what was said, not ratings the hosts published. They can be wrong. A host's score that doesn't sound right can be checked against the video in one click.

2. Taste, not hype

Everyone loving a famous game says little about taste. For each person, Taste Match first works out what they would score a game from BGG's average alone, on their own scale. The taste signal is how far their real score sits above or below that.

This also takes care of generous and harsh scorers: a host who gives lots of 5s is compared with their own usual, so the 5s alone don't make them match anyone.

3. The match

On the games you and a host both rated, the agreement is how well your taste signals line up: +1 when you always go the same way, −1 when you always go opposite ways.

The match is that agreement times how much it counts, and how much it counts grows with the evidence: the games you both rated, with low-confidence verdicts and roundup videos counting less. When a channel's team gives a verdict ("we love it"), it stands in for each host who gave none of their own in that video, at half weight times how much they were on the team then (worked out from how often each host appears, over about three months and a year): a regular host nearly fully, a one-video guest barely. Paid showcases and "most anticipated" lists don't count: they aren't verdicts on games the hosts played.

So a perfect agreement on 5 games counts the same as a fair one (+0.4) on 40, a good agreement on many games scores highest, and a clear disagreement on many games lands far below zero. To be ranked, a host needs 5 of your games: each one they rated themselves counts 1, each one only the team's verdict covers counts its weight.

The likely range on each bar is where 8 in 10 reshuffles of your shared games put the match.

4. Expansions and editions

Editions of one game (a deluxe edition, a second edition, a reprint) count as one game, on your side and the reviewers'. An expansion is its own game: a verdict on "Root: The Riverfolk" is a verdict on that expansion, not on Root.

A review often covers both, like "Chronicles of Crime and the Noir expansion". Then each game gets the verdict the host gave it: the base game's verdict counts for the base game, the expansion's for the expansion.

Sometimes the matching files an expansion's verdict under its base game (14 of about 4,200 verdicts, for example "Root: The Hirelings Expansion" under Root). Those don't count for the base game, and its page marks them "about an expansion". When a review rates both under one name, only the base game's own verdict counts.

5. What it can and can't tell you

We tested it in November 2026 on a collection of 143 rated games, hiding one game at a time and guessing it. BGG's average, put on the person's own scale, misses by 0.59 points out of 5 on average. Adding the person's taste profile (the kinds of game, designers and mechanics they rate above or below that) brings it to 0.58. Adding the reviewers' scores made the guess no better: 0.60 at best, and up to 0.72 depending on how they are added.

So Taste Match doesn't promise a forecast from the reviewers. It shows which hosts lean your way and on which games, what they actually said, and where your own taste leans, with every number traceable to a video. As more reviews are read, these tests will be run again.

6. Your data

Only your public BoardGameGeek ratings are read, through BGG's API. Nothing is posted to BGG. Your ratings and your result are kept for a day so the pages load quickly, then deleted; coming back later starts a fresh match from your current ratings.
